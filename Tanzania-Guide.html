The two routes

Both approach the summit via Barafu Camp and share a high success rate thanks to good "climb high, sleep low" acclimatization. Lemosho is longer, quieter and scenic; Machame is the classic, busier and a day shorter.

Climate zones & temperatures

You cross five ecological zones — tropical heat to arctic cold in a single climb. December is dry-season-ish: good weather, afternoon cloud, occasional rain low down, possible snow up high. Each zone shows the daytime and nighttime ranges.

Summit success is far more about altitude strategy and staying warm/dry than about fitness. These are where most climbers win or lose the mountain.

The parks — Northern Circuit

A classic post-Kilimanjaro loop out of Arusha. December is the short green season: lush scenery, newborn animals, superb birding and fewer crowds, with brief afternoon storms. The southern Serengeti (Ndutu) starts filling with the migration for calving from late December.

Climate & temperatures

Safari is warm and low-altitude compared to the mountain — but early game drives are cold, and the Ngorongoro Crater rim (~2,300 m) is genuinely chilly at dawn. December brings warm, humid days and short afternoon rains. Ranges shown day vs. night.

Dawn game drives are the cold surprise: you leave in the dark in an open vehicle at wind chill. Pack a fleece + light rain shell even though midday hits 30°C — the Ngorongoro rim can be near freezing at sunrise.

Zanzibar — the beach finish

Dec 28 – Jan 2. After the mountain and the plains, Zanzibar is the wind-down: warm Indian-Ocean beaches, Stone Town's spice-island history and reef snorkelling. Expect hot, humid days (around 29–32 °C), warm sea and the odd short shower. Clothing is easy — light, breathable, quick-dry — so this tab skips the obvious and covers what's easy to get wrong.

🎉
You're there over New Year — book early. Dec 28–Jan 2 is Zanzibar's peak week: hotels, transfers and Mnemba trips sell out and prices spike, and many resorts charge a compulsory NYE gala dinner. Also: east-coast tides pull the sea far out at low tide (check a tide table, or stay north at Nungwi / Kendwa), and Zanzibar requires visitors to hold mandatory travel insurance.
